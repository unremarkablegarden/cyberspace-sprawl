# Game design

A first pass, written to be argued with.

## What it is

A persistent city you drop into for ten minutes or two hours. Nothing to lose,
no combat, no failure state. You gather things, make things, decorate your
space, trade with people, and come back tomorrow because something changed.

## Touchstones

- **Animal Crossing**: the daily rhythm, collection, and a home that becomes
  yours through a hundred small choices.
- **Habbo Hotel**: isometric rooms, and furniture as a social economy.
- **Lucasfilm's *Habitat*** (1986), the first graphical online world. Its
  designers, Chip Morningstar and Randy Farmer, took the idea of cyberspace from
  Gibson, and their essay *The Lessons of Lucasfilm's Habitat* is still the best
  short guide to running one: you can't script a world for thousands of people,
  you can only design the economy and the tools and let them loose.
- **William Gibson's Sprawl trilogy**: *Neuromancer*, *Count Zero*, *Mona Lisa
  Overdrive*. Setting and mood only; no quoted text, no borrowed characters.

## The loop, translated

| Animal Crossing | The Sprawl |
| --- | --- |
| Tent, then house | A **coffin** in a Chiba capsule hotel, growing into a cube, a flat, a loft |
| Shells, fossils, fruit | **Scavenging**: dead decks, circuit boards, vat-grown fruit |
| Fishing, bug catching | **Data-fishing** when you jack in to the matrix |
| DIY recipes | **Schematics**, found or traded, built at a workbench |
| The museum | **The Boxmaker**: donate found objects; players assemble box collages shown in a gallery |
| The turnip market | A zaibatsu stock that swings daily |
| Bells | **New Yen** |
| Villagers | Fences and fixers |

## The world

The BAMA (Boston–Atlanta Metropolitan Axis), one district at a time, starting
in Chiba. Districts are generated from a seed, so the base city costs nothing to
store; only what players change is kept. The world grows by adding districts,
never by resetting.

## Look

Low-poly 3D seen through a fixed isometric camera and drawn at a third of screen
resolution, then scaled up with hard pixel edges and a slight colour banding.
Night, rain, neon. The camera is real 3D, so interiors or first person can come
later without new art.

## Characters

Built in code from a handful of choices (build, height, skin, hair, clothes,
colours, accessories), so every option costs a few lines and no artwork. More
parts arrive as the game grows, and later as modelled pieces.
